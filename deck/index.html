01
Product Manager case study

Getting people to buy
something new

Increasing the share of Monthly Active Customers who purchase from at least one new category each month — in Indian quick commerce.

Evidence base: 3,372 public reviews · 40 survey responses · 3 depth interviews · a deployed MVP. Every number in this deck is computed from committed data and reproducible from the repository.
02
The finding, first

Users already want to explore.
They cannot afford to.

39/40have wanted to try a new category and didn't
38/40arrive already knowing what they want
20/20split on having crossed over in 3 months

Motivation is not the constraint. Awareness is not the constraint. Trust in the platform is not the constraint — these people order several times a month.

The app shows an unfamiliar category exactly what it shows a familiar one: a price. For something you already buy, that's enough — you hold the reference point in your head. For something you never have, a price with no reference point is uninterpretable. Evaluating it costs time a two-minute fetch session hasn't budgeted, so skipping is correct.

Every skip is individually rational. The aggregate is a user who has bought the same things for two years.

Method

They were built to be compared, not averaged. Where they agree, the finding is solid. Where they disagree — and they do, sharply — the disagreement is the finding.

04
Part 1 · The engine

A pipeline, not a prompt

collect ──► normalize ──► relevance gate ──► extract ──► cluster ──► synthesize │ validate
3,372documents, 5 apps
971judged relevant (28.8%)
937extracted (96.5% of relevant)
25themes over 631 pain statements

Three design rules did the heavy lifting:

  • The schema is the source of truth. Every enum traces to one of the brief's eight questions, so coverage is checkable rather than asserted.
  • Numbers never come from the model. Prevalence and counts are computed and carried through untouched — the LLM is never shown a statistic it could inflate.
  • Contradicting evidence is computed, not requested. Ask a model to argue against itself and it finds nothing; counting label disagreement inside a cluster does.

Top information gaps, which is what the MVP had to close: freshness_expiry (198) · returnability (164) · price_benchmark (161).

07
Part 1 · Validation

The insights were checked against a second model family

Everything else in the engine is counted or verified. The mechanism behind each theme is not — it comes from a model, and "an AI wrote it" is not evidence. So synthesis was re-run on Llama 3.3 70B and compared with the Gemini output.

25/25themes synthesised by both
0.66research-question agreement (Jaccard)
0themes with zero overlap
12×mechanism matching vs chance (12/25 at 4% baseline)

Agreement is not correctness. Two models converging means they read the same quotes the same way. Models trained on overlapping data are not independent coders. What this bounds is how much the insights can be blamed on one vendor — nothing more. Disagreement would have been decisive, and there is none.

Part 1 · The limit

What 3,372 documents structurally cannot tell you

QuestionCoverageGrade

The split is not random. Everything it answers well is about a purchase that went wrong. Everything it answers badly is about behaviour before or across purchases.

A review corpus contains only people who completed a transaction. Nobody reviews a habit — a habit is the unremarkable case. Nobody reviews a product they never bought. Collecting 10,000 more documents would sharpen the top of that table and do nothing for the bottom.

09
Part 2 · Survey

Four findings that constrained the build

  • F1 — Willingness isn't the constraint. 39/40 have felt the pull and not acted. Anything premised on creating desire solves a problem the data says doesn't exist.
  • F2 — 38/40 arrive knowing what they want. Two people browse. This kills the obvious solution: a better discovery surface has almost no audience.
  • F3 — Blockers are information gaps at the moment of consideration. Not awareness. They know it's sold; they can't evaluate it fast enough to justify the risk.
  • F4 — The consideration set is already the app. Blinkit 16 / Zepto 12 / Instamart 8 vs Amazon 3. Intent already lands here; it just doesn't complete.

In Fogg terms: motivation present, prompt present, ability is the binding constraint — not affordability, but whether they can decide fast enough. That dictates what works: discounts and banners push variables already non-zero.

10
Part 2 · Interviews (n=3)

Three conversations found three things the vocabulary can't express

All three respondents, unprompted, on two different apps, named a never-bought category and explained it as irrelevance, not obstruction.

"Pet supplies, baby care never bought. I don't have kids and pets."P01
"Baby care never bought because I don't have kids."P02
"Home appliance, because I live alone and I live in full furnished so nothing to do with it."P03

None answered "where do you buy it instead?" — because nobody is buying it anywhere. All eight values in the engine's barrier[] enum presume latent demand being blocked. Forced onto the survey's closed list, all three would have been recorded as blocked, overstating addressable demand.

The other two gaps: access of last resort (2 of 3 opened the app only because nothing else was available) and basket economics — see next slide.

11
Part 2 · The sharpest result

One interview predicted the engine's own failure

"we need to buy atleast minimum rs product like 150 or 200 rs of products for not to charge delivery fees too so I never bought home appliance"P03

Unlike the other two gaps, this one leaves text — so it was checkable. Before extraction finished, the prediction written down was: the engine will either force this into price_risk or lose it.

It did both. 133 of 3,372 documents use threshold language. The relevance gate binned 64 as delivery complaints. The survivors clustered into a theme of their own — THM-05, 34 statements, labelled free / minimum / threshold / value — which the pipeline then coded price_risk, meaning "unsure whether the price is fair".

"Before it serves free delivery on 100₹ , And now 400₹"
"sudden increase in free delivery from ₹99 to ₹399 is unreasonable"Two of THM-05's own quotes

Nobody there is unsure whether a price is fair. They are re-planning a basket around a line that moved. The engine found the theme and mislabelled it, because the label it needed does not exist.

12
Part 2 · Method finding

We tested the shortcut, and it failed

Interviews are slow. The obvious substitute is generating personas from survey data — a real, current technique. So five were generated, and then tested rather than believed.

5hypotheses generated
4untestable with the available data
1testable — and it was wrong

The testable one predicted a negative relationship between order frequency and category breadth. Computed from the real rows: r = +0.25 — the opposite direction.

A persona generated from survey data cannot contain information the survey did not capture. It recombines and articulates, fluently enough to feel like insight, but it has no mechanism for being right about anything outside its input.

Read against slide 10: the synthetic method produced confident claims that failed under test, while three real conversations produced findings the survey, the personas and 3,372 mined documents could not. That is the brief's "AI insights are only a starting point", demonstrated on this project's own data rather than asserted.

13
Part 3 · The reframe

The goal is a monthly metric. One-off trial doesn't move it.

Half the survey had already crossed into a new category in three months. That looks like success until you check why the crossers still don't try more — their reasons are near-identical to the non-crossers' reasons for never trying at all. Crossing over once did not lower the barrier.

So crossover is event-triggered and non-repeating. Something external forces it, the user buys once, and reverts to their list. All three interviews confirm it, with three different triggers:

  • P01 — ran out. "I needed that very urgent."
  • P02 — urgency plus no nearby shop.
  • P03 — a life event. "i joined gym and i don't have sipper."

This changes what to build. The highest-value moment is not first trial at all — it is the window immediately after a first cross-category purchase, before the user reverts. That is a moment the platform can detect precisely.

14
Part 4 · The MVP

"One Thing" — a single card, at cart review

Not a feed, not a rail, not a browse tab. F2 rules those out: almost nobody is browsing. The card fires at cart review, when the task the user came for is already done.

Card elementBlocker it closesSurvey
Reason drawn from the user's own history"not sure it'll suit my need"14/40
Price anchor — ₹/week vs their own comparable spend"priced higher than usual"15/40
Trust signal from similar buyers"don't trust the brand"26/40
Exactly one option, no compare view"took too long to compare"13/40
Smallest available pack"too expensive"20/40

Mode B is the one that matters. Mode A is first crossover; Mode B fires when someone tried a category 2–6 weeks ago and hasn't returned. The metric counts monthly recurrence, so Mode A produces the one-off it ignores and Mode B converts it into the repeat it counts.

15
Part 4 · The hard part

The agent's most important behaviour is refusing

When the data cannot substantiate a reason, the card is not shown and the interface says why. A card whose reason is invented is exactly the noise these users already ignore — and slide 10 shows the cost precisely: a baby-care suggestion to someone with no children is a wasted impression, not a conversion opportunity.

  • Mode, category, product and price anchor are computed deterministically — identical with or without an LLM. Only the wording is generated.
  • The LLM writes copy in CI, where the key is a secret. The published site is static and holds no key; a quota outage degrades wording, never decisions.
  • Six eval assertions gate deployment. The build refuses to publish if any fail.

Deployed and live via GitHub Pages. Dismissal rate is a pre-registered guardrail — this feature is designed to be killed if it becomes noise.

16
Measurement

How we'd know it worked

Primary metric — CER (Category Expansion Rate): share of MAC purchasing from ≥1 new category this month, where "new" means unbought in a 6-month lookback.

Why Mode B is the lever:

Incremental monthly GMV = MAC × segment_share (Habitual Narrow Repeaters) × Δ(second-purchase rate) (the Mode B conversion) × avg_basket_in_category × retention_multiplier

Expressed as a formula rather than a fabricated number, so it can be run against real platform data instead of taken on faith.

Guardrails, pre-registered: card dismissal rate, session length (the fix must not make the session longer — 13/40 named comparison time as the blocker), and return/refund rate in newly-tried categories.

The strategic case is stronger than the arithmetic: a user buying one category is trivially substitutable — a competitor needs to win one habit. A user buying five has five habits to dislodge. This is a moat investment, not a GMV tactic.

17
Limitations

Stated up front, not discovered by a reader

  • 3 depth interviews against a target of 5–6. All three are non-parents and two live alone. A parent or pet owner would have tested whether "this category is irrelevant to me" is a property of these respondents or of the question. That test was not run.
  • 95% of the corpus is one platform. This is why source spread reports NOT RUN rather than a green tick.
  • Only 2 of the 7 requested source types were collected. Social APIs are paywalled; that shortfall is real.
  • The MVP runs on synthetic order histories. It demonstrates the mechanism, not real-world lift.
  • No interview was fabricated and no synthetic persona is counted toward the interview total — the personas are a labelled method artifact whose failure is itself reported.

One more, in the record deliberately: a results table in the persona analysis was wrong when first written — generated alongside the personas rather than computed. It was caught by writing a script to recompute it, and left visible with the correction, because a project arguing that plausible output is not evidence should show its own validation failing at least once.

18
What I'd do next

The three things that would change the answer

  • Add a "not relevant to me" option to the barrier taxonomy and re-field the survey. Three of three interviews hit a gap the instrument cannot record; prevalence is genuinely unknown and currently overstated.
  • Add basket_economics to the schema and re-run extraction. The signal is already collected — 133 documents — and currently mislabelled as price uncertainty, which points at the wrong fix.
  • Ship Mode B behind an experiment against the pre-registered guardrails. The argument that the post-first-purchase window is the highest-value moment is well-evidenced and still untested against real behaviour.
