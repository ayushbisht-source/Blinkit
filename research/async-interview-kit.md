# Async Interview Kit — WhatsApp, ~20 minutes of your time total

**Why async.** Scheduled calls are what makes interviews expensive. Text removes the cost: you send,
they answer whenever, you collect. Async text interviews are a recognised qualitative method — the
tradeoff is shorter answers and no live follow-up, which is a stated limitation, not a
disqualification.

**Your actual time:** ~10 min to send 6 messages. ~10 min in two days to paste replies into files.
That's it. I do the analysis.

**Target: 6 people.** Send to 10, expect 6 to reply. Anyone who orders groceries on an app a few
times a month qualifies — flatmates, cousins, colleagues, classmates, family.

---

## Step 1 — The opener (send this first, alone)

> Hey! Doing my graduation project on how people shop on Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart. Can I send you 5
> short questions on WhatsApp? Reply whenever you get a minute, no rush, no call needed — takes
> about 5 min. Would really help me out 🙏

Wait for a yes. Then send Step 2.

---

## Step 2 — The questions (paste as ONE message)

> Thanks! Here they are — just reply in whatever order, as short or long as you like:
>
> **1.** Think about the *last* order you placed on Blinkit/Zepto. What was in it, and what made you
> open the app right then?
>
> **2.** Is there a category on the app you've *never* bought from — like pet supplies, baby care,
> personal care, home stuff? Which one, and where do you buy that instead?
>
> **3.** For that category — what would actually have to be true for you to buy it on the app next
> time? Like, what would you need to know or see first?
>
> **4.** Think of the last time you bought something on the app you'd *never* bought before. What
> made you actually go through with it that time?
>
> **5.** Anything about how you shop on these apps that you think I've completely missed?

---

## Why these five

| Q | What it gets that the survey couldn't |
|---|---|
| 1 | The **trigger**. Survey asked what they buy, never what made them open the app at that moment. |
| 2 | A **named** unexplored category plus the competing channel — survey only gave aggregate counts. |
| 3 | The information gap **in their own words**. This is the single most important answer for the MVP: it tells us literally what to put on screen. |
| 4 | The mechanics of a real crossover. Tests the "event-triggered, one-off" reframe — if they describe an external trigger, it holds; if they describe curiosity, it's challenged. |
| 5 | The **escape hatch**. The only question that can surface a barrier we never thought to ask about. This is what makes it primary research rather than survey confirmation. |

Q5 is the one that earns the whole exercise. The survey was written from the discovery engine's own
vocabulary, so it can only confirm what was already hypothesised. Q5 is the one place a genuinely
unanticipated answer can appear.

---

## Step 3 — Saving replies

One file per person: `research/transcripts/P01.md` … `P06.md`

Just paste raw. Don't clean it up, don't fix typos, don't summarise — verbatim is the whole value,
and Hinglish/short replies are fine and useful.

```
# P01
Age/city (if you know): 24, Pune
Sent: 30 Jul 2026

Q1: [paste their reply]
Q2: [paste]
Q3: [paste]
Q4: [paste]
Q5: [paste]
```

No real names anywhere. P01–P06 only.

---

## If you get fewer than 6

Three is enough to be worth reporting. Two is enough to be worth quoting. The write-up states the
actual number either way — an honest n=3 with real quotes beats a claimed n=6 without them, and it
still gives Part 3 something the survey cannot: language.

---

## Follow-up (optional, costs 30 seconds)

If someone's answer to Q3 is vague ("dunno, just haven't"), one reply:

> Fair! Just to push a bit — if [category] was right there at a price you thought was fair, would
> you have tried it? Or is something else going on?

That's the closest async gets to a live probe, and it's usually where the real answer surfaces.
