## Analyst notes

### 1. Q3 refuses the question, and that refusal is the finding

> *"I will never buy from blinkit my trust issues for vegetables is still there."*

Q3 asks what would have to be **true** for the respondent to try the category. It presumes a
condition exists. P05 rejects the premise outright — not "I'd need to see freshness ratings", not
"if there were a returns guarantee", but *never*.

The cause is a **single bad experience**: *"one time I got really bad vegetables."* One delivery,
and the category is closed permanently.

This matters for the root cause. `docs/03-problem-definition.md` §2 frames the barrier as an
information deficit — the user cannot evaluate an unfamiliar category fast enough, so supplying the
missing reference point unlocks it. **P05 is not short of information.** They have information: they
tried it, it was bad, and they updated. No price anchor, freshness badge or trust signal addresses a
belief formed by direct experience. The MVP would show this user a vegetables card and be correctly
ignored.

P05 is therefore the second respondent of five whose barrier is **not informational** — after P04,
whose grocery block resolves only if they move house or the kirana closes. Two of five is enough to
say the Fogg-ability model does not cover the whole population, and `docs/03` should stop implying
it does.

### 2. This independently confirms the engine's single largest theme

The corpus's biggest clusters are about exactly this. **THM-01** (72 statements, 11.4%) —
*receives / expiry / spoiled / milk*. **THM-04** (42) and **THM-02** (51) — produce and quality
failures, with representative quotes including *"kharab sade huye vegetables delivery"* and *"send me
spoiled vegetables or curdled milk when I am raising the issue with prove"*.

The engine established that spoiled-produce complaints dominate the corpus. It could not establish
what a reviewer *does next*, because a review is one moment and the corpus is cross-sectional.
**P05 supplies the missing half: the category is abandoned permanently, from one incident.** That is
the mechanism behind the volume, and it is exactly the kind of claim the discovery engine is
structurally unable to make on its own (`docs/01-discovery-engine.md`, the coverage table).

Read together, the two sources are stronger than either: 3,372 documents establish prevalence, one
interview establishes consequence.

### 3. Q4 answers a question P03 raised

P03 never bought home appliances — *"I live alone and I live in full furnished so nothing to do with
it."* P05 bought home appliances — *"as we shifted to our new home."*

Same category, opposite outcomes, and the difference is entirely **life stage**. Neither respondent
was blocked by anything the app controls: one had no need, the other acquired one. This is the
cleanest illustration in the study of why a barrier taxonomy that presumes latent demand
misdescribes people — the category was dormant for P03 and activated for P05 by an event no
interface could cause.

It is also the **fifth consecutive event-triggered crossover**, and the second driven by a life
event specifically (P03's gym membership was the first).

Note the co-factor, as with P04: *"in a very good price."* Price accompanies the trigger without
being it. Across five respondents the pattern is consistent — an event opens the category, price
confirms the purchase. The one exception is P03's Q1, where a ₹200 discount was the trigger with no
event at all.

### 4. Q1 and Q5 place P05 with the majority

*"very easy to buy from blinkit and very good prices"* · *"I shop mostly regularly."* Three of five
respondents now self-describe as regular shoppers (P02, P03, P05) against one need-driven (P01),
with P04 describing simple, unelaborated shopping. **Still nobody has described browsing.** The
standing prediction — that one respondent describing genuine browsing would put the fetch-mode model
in trouble — survives a fifth interview.

## Coding against `engine/schema.py`

| Field | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `habit_driver` | `price_certainty`, `satisficing` | Q1 — "very easy", "very good prices" |
| `barrier` | **`trust_quality`** | Q2 — explicit, experience-based. Clean enum fit |
| `discovery_channel` | `search` | arrives decided |
| `information_gap` | *(none applicable — see note)* | no information is being sought; the belief is settled |
| `segment_signal` | — | recent mover; no enum value covers household transition |
| `habit_signal` | 1 | *"I shop mostly regularly"* |
| `pain_statement` | Abandoned a category permanently after one bad delivery, and no in-app information will reopen it because the belief comes from direct experience rather than uncertainty. | Q2 + Q3 |

`barrier` fits cleanly here, but `information_gap` does not — and the pair is the point. The schema
assumes that naming a barrier implies an information need that could close it. `trust_quality`
formed by experience has no corresponding gap, and the schema has no way to say so.

## Against the engine, survey, and P01–P04

| Claim | P05 |
|---|---|
| `trust_quality` is the dominant barrier (engine 45.3%, survey top blocker) | **Confirmed, and given a mechanism** — one bad delivery closes the category permanently |
| Crossover is event-triggered (`docs/03` §5) | **Confirmed** — moving house; 5 of 5 across the study |
| The binding constraint is evaluation speed (Fogg ability) | **Challenged** — the belief is experience-based; no information reopens it |
| Barriers imply an information gap that could close them | **Challenged** — clean `barrier` code, no applicable `information_gap` |
| Price triggers crossover | **Challenged** — price co-occurs with the event, does not cause it |
| Users arrive knowing what they want (F2) | **Confirmed** |
| Nobody browses | **Confirmed** — 5 of 5, standing prediction still unbroken |

**Caveat:** n=1 for a permanent refusal. One respondent saying "never" does not establish how many
users abandon a category after a single bad delivery — that is a churn question answerable from
platform data (repeat-purchase rate in a category following a refund or complaint) and not from five
interviews. What P05 establishes is that the mechanism exists and that the current root-cause model
does not account for it.
