# Discussion Guide — 30 minutes

Compressed from 60. The cuts are deliberate: concept reaction is trimmed hardest, because the
diagnostic sections (2–4) are what Part 3 is built from.

**Three rules, and they matter more than the questions:**
1. Never say your hypothesis out loud. If you mention "trying new categories" in the first 20
   minutes, everything after it is contaminated.
2. Ask about **the last time**, never "usually." People describe an idealised self when asked what
   they usually do.
3. Count to three after they stop talking. The second thing they say is the honest one.

---

### 1. Warm-up — 3 min
- Tell me about the last thing you ordered on Blinkit/Zepto.
- What was going on that made you open the app right then?

*Listening for: trigger type — running out, planned restock, sudden need, boredom.*

### 2. Order-history walkthrough — 8 min
**Ask them to open the app and read out their last 3–4 orders.**

- Walk me through this one. What made you add each thing?
- Is this pretty typical of what a month looks like for you?
- How much of this is you re-ordering versus deciding fresh each time?
- *(if they use a saved list / reorder button)* Tell me about that — when did you start doing it that way?

*Listening for: `habit_driver` — time-pressure, trust, satisficing, list-reuse, price-certainty.
This section produces your best quotes. Don't rush it.*

### 3. The unexplored category — 10 min
Pick a category they've clearly never touched, ideally one their life-stage suggests they *should*
(pet food if they mentioned a dog; baby care if they mentioned a kid; personal care for anyone).

- I noticed you've never ordered [X] here. Where do you get that?
- Walk me through the last time you bought [X] — wherever that was.
- What would have to be true for you to buy it on this app instead?
- *(after they answer)* What would you need to know before you'd risk it?
- Has the app ever shown you [X]? What did you think when you saw it?

*Listening for: `barrier[]` and `information_gap[]`. Do not name any barrier — make them supply it.
The last question is the one that separates "never saw it" from "saw it, ignored it," and that
distinction is the entire problem statement.*

### 4. Discovery + workarounds — 6 min
- Think of the last time you bought something genuinely new on one of these apps. How did that happen?
- When you need something you've never bought on the app, what do you do instead?
- Who or what do you trust when you're trying a product you don't know?

*Listening for: `discovery_channel[]` and workarounds. Workarounds are the strongest proof a need is
real — people only build them for problems they actually have.*

### 5. Concept reaction — 3 min
Only now, and only briefly. Describe one concept in a sentence, watch the face, ask "what's your
first reaction?" Do not defend it. Do not explain it twice.

---

## After each interview — 5 minutes, do it immediately

Write to `research/notes/P0N.md`:
- Top 3 quotes, verbatim
- Which insight IDs this confirmed / challenged
- One thing that surprised you

The surprise field is the most valuable line in the whole research phase. It's where the interviews
earn their place against the AI engine — and "nothing surprised me" after six interviews usually
means the guide was leading, not that the engine was right.
